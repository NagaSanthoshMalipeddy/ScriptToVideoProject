What happens when two powerful neighbors go to war for eight years?

It happened between Iran and Iraq.

The year was 1980.

Iran had just gone through the Islamic Revolution, and Iraq's leader Saddam Hussein saw an opportunity to challenge Iran.

On September 22, 1980, Iraqi forces launched a major invasion of Iran.

Watch the map.

The Iraqi flag moves across the Iranian border, while Iraqi forces attack several areas in western Iran.

But Iraq's advance did not lead to a quick victory.

Iran reorganized its forces and pushed back.

By 1982, Iranian forces had driven Iraqi troops out of most of the Iranian territory they had captured.

But instead of ending the war, the conflict continued.

The front lines shifted back and forth.

Both countries attacked military and economic targets, including oil infrastructure.

The war also became notorious for the use of chemical weapons, particularly by Iraq.

And then came the final phase.

After eight years of devastating fighting, both sides accepted a UN-backed ceasefire.

August 20, 1988.

The Iran-Iraq War was finally over.

Eight years.

Hundreds of thousands of lives lost.

And no clear battlefield victory.

But the story doesn't end there…

Because this war completely changed the Middle East.
