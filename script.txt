# HOOK
[MAP: satellite zoom from East Asia to the Korean Peninsula]
[TEXT POP: "70+ YEARS"]
70+ Years Later, North & South Korea Are STILL at War!

[BORDER GLOW: Korean Demilitarized Zone]
It sounds like clickbait. South Korea is a global economic power. North Korea is a nuclear-armed state. Both have existed for generations.

[TEXT POP: "NO PEACE TREATY"]
But the war that created this divide never received a final peace treaty. The shooting mostly stopped. The legal and political conflict did not.

# SETUP
[CAMERA: slow orbit above Korea]
To understand why, watch this narrow peninsula. It sits between China, Russia, and Japan, facing the Pacific.

[PIN: Seoul · Pyongyang]
Today, Seoul and Pyongyang are only about 195 kilometres apart. Yet crossing the border is almost impossible for ordinary people.

[MAP: India to Korea comparison route]
For viewers in India, imagine 2 rival states separated by a fortified strip, while families and history continue across it.

[TIMELINE: 1910 → 1945]
The story begins before either modern Korea existed. Japan annexed Korea in 1910 and ruled it until 1945.

# BODY
[TIMELINE: August 1945]
Japan surrendered at the end of World War Two. Therefore, Korea was suddenly free from Japanese rule.

[BORDER GLOW: 38th parallel]
But freedom did not bring one Korean government. Soviet forces entered from the north. American forces entered from the south.

[TEXT POP: "38° NORTH"]
Officials used the 38th parallel as a temporary dividing line. It was chosen quickly, not by the Korean people.

[FLAG: USSR north · USA south]
Therefore, the Soviet Union administered the north. The United States administered the south.

[TIMELINE: 1948]
But Cold War distrust grew. Instead of reunification, 2 rival governments appeared in 1948.

[TEXT POP: "DPRK"]
In the north, Kim Il Sung led the Democratic People's Republic of Korea.

[TEXT POP: "ROK"]
In the south, Syngman Rhee led the Republic of Korea.

[VS CARD: both claim all Korea]
Both governments claimed to represent the entire peninsula. Therefore, the border was never accepted as permanent.

[TIMELINE: June 25, 1950]
[SFX: boom]
Then, on June 25, 1950, North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel. The Korean War began.

[ROUTE: red arrows toward Seoul]
The attack moved fast. Seoul fell within 3 days.

[ROUTE: red arrows toward Busan]
Therefore, South Korean forces retreated. United Nations forces, led mainly by the United States, joined the defense.

[PIN: Busan]
By August, defenders held a shrinking area around Busan, called the Pusan Perimeter.

[TEXT POP: "ALMOST THE WHOLE SOUTH"]
North Korean forces controlled most of the peninsula. It looked like the war might end quickly.

[PIN: Incheon]
[SFX: whoosh]
But on September 15, 1950, UN forces landed at Incheon, far behind the front.

[ARROW: supply lines cut]
The landing threatened North Korean supply lines. Therefore, the entire battlefield changed.

[PIN: Seoul]
Seoul was retaken. UN and South Korean forces crossed north of the 38th parallel.

[ROUTE: blue arrows toward Pyongyang]
They captured Pyongyang and moved toward the Yalu River, beside China.

[MAP: zoom to China–Korea border]
But China feared hostile forces near its border. Chinese forces entered the war in large numbers.

[ROUTE: orange arrows south]
Therefore, UN forces were pushed south. Seoul changed hands again in January 1951.

[ARROW: line returns north]
But UN forces regrouped and retook Seoul in March.

[ARROW: front line oscillates]
The front moved north. Then south. Then north again.

[TEXT POP: "STALEMATE"]
By mid-1951, neither side could force a clear victory. Therefore, the war became a stalemate near the old dividing line.

[TIMELINE: July 1951]
Peace talks began in July 1951. But the fighting did not stop while negotiators argued.

[TEXT POP: "2 MORE YEARS"]
For 2 more years, soldiers fought over hills while talks continued.

[VS CARD: prisoners · border · supervision]
The hardest questions involved prisoners of war, the new boundary, and how any agreement would be monitored.

[TIMELINE: July 27, 1953]
[SFX: ding]
Finally, on July 27, 1953, the Korean Armistice Agreement was signed at Panmunjom.

[TEXT POP: "FIGHTING STOPS"]
North Korea, China, and the United Nations Command signed it. The agreement stopped active fighting.

[TEXT POP: "SOUTH KOREA DID NOT SIGN"]
But South Korea's government refused to sign. It opposed a settlement that left Korea divided.

[TEXT POP: "ARMISTICE ≠ PEACE"]
More importantly, an armistice is not a peace treaty. It pauses a war without fully ending the dispute.

[MAP: DMZ line appears]
Therefore, the agreement created a military boundary and a Demilitarized Zone around it.

[TEXT POP: "ABOUT 250 km"]
The DMZ stretches about 250 kilometres across the peninsula.

[TEXT POP: "ABOUT 4 km WIDE"]
It is roughly 4 kilometres wide, with each side pulling back about 2 kilometres from the military line.

[PIN: Panmunjom]
Panmunjom became the symbol of the frozen conflict. Meetings happen there, but normal travel does not.

[SPLIT SCREEN: family photos north / south]
The human cost continued after the battles. Millions of relatives were separated by the division.

[CLOCK: decades pass]
Some families later met in limited reunions. But many people died without seeing relatives again.

[VS CARD: Seoul today · Pyongyang today]
Meanwhile, the 2 Koreas developed in radically different ways.

[CHART: South Korea economy rises]
South Korea became a democracy and one of the world's largest economies.

[CHART: North Korea isolation]
North Korea remained under one-party rule, built a large military, and later developed nuclear weapons.

[BORDER GLOW: DMZ]
Therefore, the armistice line became more than a battlefield marker. It became one of Earth's sharpest political divides.

[TIMELINE: 1972 → 2000 → 2018]
There have been talks, summits, family reunions, and moments of reduced tension.

[SPLIT SCREEN: handshake · missile launch]
But periods of hope repeatedly gave way to missile tests, military drills, sanctions, and renewed threats.

[TEXT POP: "NOT DAILY WARFARE"]
This does not mean the 2 Koreas fight every day. Most days pass without shooting.

[TEXT POP: "AN UNFINISHED WAR"]
But without a peace treaty, the Korean War remains formally unfinished.

# AHA
[CAMERA: pull out to East Asia]
So why are North and South Korea still described as being at war?

[VS CARD: ceasefire vs peace]
Because the 1953 agreement was a ceasefire between military commanders, not a final political peace between the 2 Korean states.

[TEXT POP: "PAUSED IN 1953"]
That is the surprising truth. The war did not reach a normal ending. It was paused in 1953.

[MAP: both Koreas highlighted in gold]
More than 70 years later, the pause still shapes alliances, armies, diplomacy, and millions of lives.

[TEXT POP: "ONE PENINSULA · TWO STATES"]
One peninsula became 2 states. A temporary line became a fortified border. And a ceasefire became a lifetime.

# CTA
[CAMERA: slow pullback over Asia]
History is not only what ended. Sometimes, it is what the world never finished.
