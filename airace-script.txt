AI is changing the world, but there's a race happening behind the scenes. And these countries are fighting to control the future of artificial intelligence.

First, the United States. It dominates the AI software ecosystem, with companies like OpenAI, Microsoft, Google and NVIDIA driving much of the industry.

Then there's China. It's investing heavily in AI infrastructure, chips, robotics and large language models, while building its own technology ecosystem.

But AI needs something incredibly important: chips. And that puts Taiwan at the center of the race.

South Korea and Japan are also major players, especially in semiconductors, memory, electronics and robotics.

And India is taking a different route, building AI talent, digital infrastructure and domestic AI capabilities at massive scale.

But here's the interesting part: no single country controls the entire AI stack.

One country might dominate chips, another computing, another AI models, and another the applications.

So the real question isn't who wins the AI race, but who controls the most important pieces of it?
