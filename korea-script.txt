June 25, 1950.

The Korean War begins.

North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel and invaded South Korea, with the backing of the Soviet Union.

The North Korean army rapidly advanced south and captured Seoul.

But South Korea was supported by a United Nations coalition led by the United States.

UN forces pushed North Korean troops back, advancing deep into North Korea.

Then China entered the war, pushing UN forces back toward the south.

After years of fighting, the front eventually stabilized near the 38th parallel.

In 1953, an armistice was signed.

But here's the shocking part.

North and South Korea never signed a peace treaty.

So technically, the Korean War never formally ended.
